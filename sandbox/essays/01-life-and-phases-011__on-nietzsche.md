4.  **god's fall into the ridiculous.** bataille describes proust's "teacup" as symbolizing "god's fall (the fall of transcendence) into the ridiculous (into immediacy and immanence)—that teacup becomes impalement" (part iii, feb-apr iv). this is a powerful inversion: the ultimate transcendent ideal ("god") is shown to collapse into the utterly trivial and material ("teacup"), becoming the very instrument of a profound, painful descent ("impalement").

5.  **the liberating laughter at god.** confronted with "god, before whom multitudes trembled" (an upward movement of awe and power), bataille's response is to "laugh innocently and divinely" (part iii, feb-apr i). this move is not a negation, but a redirection of reverence into joyous, blasphemous simplicity, "giving back to nothingness what is simply nothingness." the awe-inspiring is reduced to the comical and the empty, allowing a different kind of freedom.

6.  **transcendence as a mask of hypocrisy.** bataille observes that "transcending the masses is like spitting in the air: what you spit out falls back on you... transcendence (noble existence, moral disdain, an attitude of sublimity) has declined, becoming hypocrisy" (august 1944). the upward move of claiming a higher moral or social ground is inverted by revealing its true low term: self-serving pretense and a return to the very base materialism of the individual's own spittle.
