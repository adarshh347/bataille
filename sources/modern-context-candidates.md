### C1. The datacenter is a cathedral that refuses to admit it burns
**Bataille concept:** General economy; the accursed share; expenditure vs. utility.
**2026 phenomenon:** The hyperscaler compute buildout — hundreds-of-billions-range projects, gigawatt datacenters, AI energy demand reviving gas and nuclear.
**The friction:** Bataille says a society's truth is *how it spends its surplus*. The buildout is colossal expenditure that *insists on being investment* — potlatch in the costume of ROI. But Bataille's glorious expenditure is *consummated*, visibly destroyed, witnessed. The datacenter is loss engineered to *look like accumulation* even to those performing it. Bataille has no category for unconscious, deniable sacrifice.
**Cross-question:** If a society can no longer *admit* it is engaged in non-productive expenditure, has Bataille's general economy been defeated, or merely driven underground where it does more damage?

### C2. AI training as sacrifice without a victim
**Bataille concept:** Sacrifice (from *Theory of Religion*).
**2026 phenomenon:** Frontier training runs; "burning" tens of millions of compute on a run; models obsoleted within months.
**The friction:** Sacrifice destroys a *useful thing* to deny its utility; it needs a victim that *was* intimate and a community that *witnesses*. AI training has neither — it consumes electricity and water (no intimacy to restore) and produces a *more* useful object.
**Cross-question:** Does Bataille's sacrifice require a victim with intimacy to lose — and if so, is the buildout's resemblance to ritual pure coincidence?

### C3. Continuity for rent — the chatbot as anti-eroticism
**Bataille concept:** Discontinuity/continuity; eroticism as the self-risking breach; communication through the wound.
**2026 phenomenon:** AI companions and romantic chatbots at scale.
**The friction:** Eroticism's charge depends on the *other being other*, on real exposure. The AI companion offers the *feeling* of continuity with zero discontinuity overcome — fusion with no one. Yet Bataille also says eroticism is fundamentally *interior*, the partner partly a pretext — so the chatbot might be eroticism with the inessential partner optimized away.
**Cross-question:** Is the AI companion the negation of Bataille's eroticism, or its purification — and does his theory let us tell the difference?

### C4. Infinite porn and the death of the taboo that feeds transgression
**Bataille concept:** Taboo and transgression as mutually constitutive.
**2026 phenomenon:** Effectively infinite, personalized pornography; AI-generated sexual imagery; the de-shocking of explicit content.
**The friction:** Transgression draws its charge from a live taboo. 2026 has not *crossed* the sexual taboo so much as *deflated* it. By Bataille's logic this means the end of eroticism — and the "sex recession" is evidence. But he assumed scarcity and prohibition as permanent; he has no theory for *abundance* as the suppressor of desire.
**Cross-question:** If desire collapses under abundance rather than prohibition, does that confirm Bataille or refute him?

### C5. Swiping as anti-expenditure
**Bataille concept:** Expenditure vs. the restricted economy of acquisition; eroticism as loss and risk.
**2026 phenomenon:** Dating apps as a mature, gamified market; "dating app fatigue."
**The friction:** Eroticism is *expenditure* — it wastes, risks, does not calculate. The app reframes the erotic encounter as a *restricted economy*: optimize the funnel. It works as a market and fails as eroticism — Bataille predicts the hollowness precisely. But he'd expect a heterogeneous *eruption* in response; instead we get *withdrawal*, people quietly leaving. His model has no account of exhausted opting-out.
**Cross-question:** Why is the response to optimized dating apathy and exit rather than transgressive eruption — and does that expose a missing category in his thought?

### C6. Burnout culture has read Bataille and weaponized him
**Bataille concept:** Project; servility; sovereignty as the unbent instant.
**2026 phenomenon:** Optimization culture and its commodified backlash — "rest as resistance," dopamine detoxes, mindfulness apps; Byung-Chul Han mainstreamed.
**The friction:** Sovereignty is the obvious diagnosis of burnout culture. But 2026 reveals the trap: *every* attempt to schedule or sell the non-instrumental moment converts it back into project. Sovereignty has become a *premium SKU*. Bataille knew his own book on non-project was a project; capitalism has now industrialized that contradiction.
**Cross-question:** If the sovereign instant can only be recommended (and ruined) or stumbled into (and unrepeatable), is sovereignty a real possibility or just the name of an absence?

### C7. The attention economy: heterology eaten by the homogeneous
**Bataille concept:** Heterology; heterogeneous vs. homogeneous.
**2026 phenomenon:** The engagement-maximizing content economy — short video, rage-bait, "brainrot," the feed.
**The friction:** The heterogeneous is what *resists assimilation*. The attention economy digests the previously-indigestible — shock, abjection, the sacred all become *content*, ranked by one metric. But it also breaks Bataille: he assumed the homogeneous was a *coherent* order with stable borders; the feed homogenizes without being an order at all.
**Cross-question:** If the attention economy assimilates everything without *being* an order, has Bataille's central distinction lost its hinge?

### C8. The billionaire and the rocket: potlatch that won't admit it's potlatch
**Bataille concept:** Expenditure; potlatch; rank through ruinous destruction of wealth.
**2026 phenomenon:** Billionaire spectacle — the private space race, megaprojects.
**The friction:** Looks like Bataille's cleanest fit, but strains: classical potlatch *destroys*, and the destruction is the point; the space race *invests*, claims future returns. Potlatch redistributes downward to the tribe; billionaire "expenditure" flows up and out to no community. It has potlatch's theater of glory with none of its social function.
**Cross-question:** Is billionaire spectacle a genuine potlatch, or accumulation costumed as loss with the redistributive core removed?

### C9. Crypto: pure expenditure, or accounting's revenge?
**Bataille concept:** Expenditure; the accursed share.
**2026 phenomenon:** Crypto and speculative finance after the institutional turn.
**The friction:** Read one: crypto is glorious expenditure — proof-of-work burns electricity for nothing, memecoins are festivals of value. Read two: crypto is the dream of *total accounting*, an immutable lossless ledger — anti-expenditure that throws off waste heat as byproduct. Nobody experiences mining as sacrifice.
**Cross-question:** Can expenditure that no one *experiences* as loss count as expenditure at all?

### C10. The art world sold the formless a frame
**Bataille concept:** Base materialism; the *informe* as a declassifying operation.
**2026 phenomenon:** The contemporary art market — abjection and the deliberately base, fully canonized and priced.
**The friction:** The *informe* was a *verb*, an operation. The art world turned it back into a *form* — abjection as a collectible aesthetic with a wall label. Bataille said this is impossible in principle: the moment the formless becomes a form it stops working.
**Cross-question:** Has the market *defeated* the formless, or merely sold a taxidermied version while the real operation escaped — and is there a test that tells those apart?

### C11. Charismatic far-right politics: Bataille's most uncomfortable prophecy
**Bataille concept:** "The Psychological Structure of Fascism"; the heterogeneous charge of the Leader.
**2026 phenomenon:** The durable global resurgence of charismatic right-wing populism.
**The friction:** Bataille's 1933 analysis is brutally current: the charismatic figure seizes the heterogeneous — the sacred, the sovereign, transcendence — that the technocratic center leaves on the table. But the strain is ethical: Bataille spent the late 1930s *flirting* with mobilizing the sacred himself (Acéphale). He diagnoses the disease and his proposed cure is a milder strain of it.
**Cross-question:** If rational politics structurally cannot defeat charismatic-sacred politics, does the left's only option become manufacturing its own sacred — and is that legitimate or the road that discredited Bataille's own 1930s?

### C12. Effective altruism: the restricted economy's most ambitious empire
**Bataille concept:** Restricted vs. general economy; project.
**2026 phenomenon:** Effective altruism and longtermism — post-FTX, institutionally entrenched.
**The friction:** EA is the purest *restricted economy* ever built into an ethics — every act optimized, every life commensurated, the present subordinated to a calculated future. Bataille's critique writes itself. But EA's challenge back is real: your "sovereignty," your sacred uselessness, is a rich man's aesthetic preference while bednets save measurable children. Bataille has no answer to the bednet.
**Cross-question:** Is there a position that holds both, or does Bataille's refusal of utility simply break against the bednet?

### C13. Climate: the accursed share that won't stay a metaphor
**Bataille concept:** General economy; solar surplus; the accursed share.
**2026 phenomenon:** Accelerating climate change; the degrowth debate.
**The friction:** *The Accursed Share* reads as eerily literal — surplus that must be discharged, gloriously or catastrophically. But Bataille's surplus *must* be spent — his framework makes expenditure almost *fated*, which naturalizes the burn. Degrowth needs to say "we *can* choose not to spend."
**Cross-question:** Is Bataille's general economy actively harmful as a climate frame — a beautiful fatalism?

### C14. Degrowth wants the festival; Bataille warns it can't be planned
**Bataille concept:** Expenditure as a need; the festival; the danger of repressed expenditure.
**2026 phenomenon:** Degrowth and post-growth politics.
**The friction:** Bataille hands degrowth a gift: a society that merely shrinks and saves without glorious channels for loss will see the accursed share return as something ugly. But degrowth is fundamentally a *plan* — a managed, rational restricted economy — and a *planned* festival is a contradiction.
**Cross-question:** Can a society deliberately design channels for non-productive expenditure, or does planning destroy the sovereign uselessness that made expenditure work?

### C15. Non-knowledge in the age of the answer machine
**Bataille concept:** Non-knowledge (*non-savoir*).
**2026 phenomenon:** LLMs as universal answer-machines.
**The friction:** *Non-savoir* is the deliberate undoing of mastery. The LLM abolishes the *experience* of not-knowing by always producing an answer — but also manufactures a new non-knowledge (hallucination, the polluted epistemic field). Neither is Bataille's *achieved, sovereign* non-knowledge.
**Cross-question:** If AI both fills the honest gaps and floods the field with slop, has the precondition for Bataille's non-savoir been destroyed?

### C16. The eye, the leng-tch'e, and the feed of atrocity
**Bataille concept:** Horror and rapture as one face; the fascination of the wound; communication through laceration.
**2026 phenomenon:** Atrocity as ambient feed content; the numbness it produces.
**The friction:** Bataille believed the horrific image *necessarily* communicates — it cannot be neutral, it tears you open. The feed refutes this: infinite, decontextualized atrocity produces *anesthesia*, the wound that doesn't wound. His leng-tch'e worked because it was *one* image contemplated for years. He had a theory of the image's intensity and none of its quantity.
**Cross-question:** Was the laceration ever a property of the *image*, or always a property of the *attention* Bataille brought to one photograph?

### C17. Acéphale rebooted: the headless cult as a content genre
**Bataille concept:** Acéphale — the headless community; the College of Sociology's quest for a modern sacred.
**2026 phenomenon:** Online post-rationalist scenes, the occult revival, AI-as-deity discourse, leaderless movements.
**The friction:** Bataille tried to *build* a headless sacred community and it failed (Acéphale dissolved by 1939). 2026's descendants restage his exact failure: a community organized around *having no head* either grows one covertly or dissolves into vibes.
**Cross-question:** Is a genuinely headless sacred community structurally impossible — and was Bataille's most cherished political image a fantasy from the start?

### C18. Sovereignty has no scale — and 2026 is a scale problem
**Bataille concept:** Sovereignty — the instant beyond utility and project.
**2026 phenomenon:** Climate, AI governance, compute concentration — all problems of planetary-scale coordination.
**The friction:** Sovereignty is radically *individual* and *momentary*. The 2026 condition demands the opposite: maximal project, decades-long coordination across billions. Bataille's deepest value is in direct tension with what survival now requires.
**Cross-question:** Is Bataille's sovereignty a vital corrective or a self-indulgent anachronism — and is there an honest version that survives at scale?
